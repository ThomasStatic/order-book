# Design Notes

Design notes and reports. The matching rules and invariants themselves are documented where they are enforced, in `include/order_book/order_book.hpp` and `include/order_book/invariants.hpp`.

- [`replay-format.md`](replay-format.md): the CSV command format for replaying a session through the book, through three exact headers (v3 names each command's instrument): columns, field syntax, sequence ordering, which layer rejects what, and the fail-fast error policy; then the replay engine, its statistics and summary, and `order_book_cli replay`. Sample files and expected output are in `tests/replay/`.
- [`command-log-format.md`](command-log-format.md): the binary command log, byte for byte: header, records, the checksum chain, positions, extension, and errors.
- [`checkpoint-format.md`](checkpoint-format.md): the checkpoint file and the book image it wraps, byte for byte: the file's header, sections, and checksum, every refusal `decodeCheckpoint` makes, and what `checkpoint verify` and `checkpoint describe` print; then the image's header, level, order, and stop records, their canonical order, extension, and every refusal `restore` makes with its offset. Golden samples are in `tests/checkpoint/`.
- [`OPERATIONS.md`](OPERATIONS.md): the operator's guide to `order_book_engine`: starting it, the command log and its two durability levels, the directory lock and recovery, the torn writes recovery cuts, what to do when recovery stops (`order_book_cli verify`, then maybe `cut-log`, and a poison command), offline `checkpoint roll`, changing placement after a clean stop, a clean stop that exits 7, why a real power cut cannot be tested here, gateway threads, pinning and priority, the stops on each platform and why closing a Windows console window can cut a clean stop short, the exit codes, the stop report, and the abort line.
- [`wire-protocol.md`](wire-protocol.md): how a client talks to the engine, byte for byte: frames, the entry check, and every code. Only a refused command is safe to retry. `order_book_cli send` is a client of it.
- [`BENCHMARKS.md`](BENCHMARKS.md): the recorded benchmark baselines. v2.1.0's engine set comes first: the gate against v2.0.0 and its one accepted exception, scaling to six shards, the kinds side by side, producers, gateway threads and siblings, durability and the roll worker, books, spreads, and the gateway's window. Then the v2.0.0 baseline and the first one, which percentiles are and are not reported and why, methodology, and limitations. Raw results are in `benchmarks/results/`.
- [`LATENCY.md`](LATENCY.md): the latency report and the claims rule every latency statement follows: what a report may say and what it must refuse. It holds v2.1.0's recorded set: command, hand-off, durable-wait, and round-trip rows, closed loop and against rate, waking an idle engine, the zero-heap before and after, and what can exceed them. It also keeps V1's three decision rows (spin window, idle connections, and the 10k/s durable wait beside a raw write-through loop) and what each rule decided.
- [`PROFILING.md`](PROFILING.md): where the baseline spends its time, from a sampling profile and exact allocation counts, and the three strongest bottleneck hypotheses.
- [`OPTIMIZATION-EXPERIMENTS.md`](OPTIMIZATION-EXPERIMENTS.md): one designed experiment per candidate optimization, the paired measurement protocol, and each experiment's measured outcome, including the reverted ones.
- [`internals/`](internals/): contributor notes by topic, the engine's contracts and the conventions of each test suite, indexed by the root `CLAUDE.md`.
- [`images/`](images/): the figures in the README and these reports. `make_figures.py` redraws the data-driven ones (the benchmark baselines, engine scaling, engine latency, optimization, replay terminal) from the result files and the CLI's expected output; the four diagrams are edited as SVG.
- [`OPTIMIZATION.md`](OPTIMIZATION.md): the optimization report: what profiling revealed, before-and-after measurements and profiles, one kept and one rejected change in detail, the readability trade-offs, what remains unoptimized, and how correctness was checked.
